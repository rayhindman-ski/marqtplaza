# BR-12 — Dutch-English localization parity

## Enhancement assessment

**Priority:** P1 — High

**Criticality:** High

**Complexity:** High

**Estimated effort:** 10–18 person-days (provisional)

This estimate covers the full existing implementation specification, not only copy: string and dynamic-state inventory, governed terminology, locale routing and persistence, formatting and plurals, accessible names and metadata, all discovery/privacy/account states, responsive layout, automated coverage checks, and acceptance testing. Risk is high because mixed or semantically unequal language can change privacy, trust, or task outcomes; complexity is high because parity spans static content, runtime data, routes, integrations, and every exceptional state.

**Work packages**

- Localization inventory, glossary, and parity design: **2–3 person-days**
- Locale infrastructure, routing, and formatting implementation: **3–5 person-days**
- Dutch-English static and dynamic content integration: **3–6 person-days**
- Automated coverage, layout, accessibility, and parity QA: **2–4 person-days**

**Estimation basis and exclusions:** The range excludes global rollout or dataset acquisition, ongoing verification operations, and third-party or legal lead times. Existing core search, authentication, and data contracts are presumed reusable; if they are absent or materially incompatible, re-estimate. Person-days are combined design, engineering, and QA effort, not elapsed delivery time, and overlap across BRs must not be summed blindly. ## Status and interpretation

This document defines proposed requirements, not implemented or verified behavior.
The live consumer application, localization framework, content store, routing, APIs, locale negotiation, and translation workflow are unknown and must be inspected during implementation.
The workspace report viewer and weekend guide are not the live consumer application.
Examples and proposed interfaces below must be reconciled with existing terminology and contracts.

## Exact business requirement

> The experience must provide complete Dutch-English parity and consistent geographical terminology.

## Objective

Give Dutch- and English-language consumers equivalent discovery capabilities, information, state feedback, privacy explanations, and account journeys.
Use consistent locale-appropriate naming for The Hague: **Den Haag** in Dutch and **The Hague** in English, while using a stable language-neutral identifier internally.
Preserve locale with validated search criteria in canonical shared URLs and browser history.
Prevent mixed-language screens, untranslated dynamic statuses, or different functional outcomes by locale.
Treat new copy in both locales as a single delivery unit.

## Scope

- Consumer navigation, value proposition, search, neighbourhood browse, filters, results, details, map opt-in, trust, privacy, accounts, favourites, errors, and empty states.
- Static and dynamic copy, accessible names, alternative text, status announcements, metadata, dates, times, numbers, and pluralization.
- Hague city and neighbourhood terminology, while preserving official proper names where translation is inappropriate.
- Locale detection, explicit switching, URL persistence, fallback, and integration payloads.
- Responsive layout impact of translation at mobile and desktop sizes.
- Translation quality, automated coverage checks, and release gates.

## Non-goals

- Adding languages beyond Dutch and English in this requirement.
- Machine-translating source facts or merchant names without a governed content decision.
- Translating external websites, map-provider UI outside available configuration, or browser permission dialogs.
- Changing canonical entity IDs when display language changes.
- Claiming availability outside current Hague-only coverage.
- Using the desktop screenshot as proof of translation completeness.
- Allowing one locale to ship a feature before equivalent copy and behavior exist in the other.

## Terminology policy

Use **Den Haag** in Dutch consumer copy and **The Hague** in English consumer copy.
Use a stable internal city ID, proposed as an existing canonical ID discovered during implementation; do not assume a new literal.
Keep official business, venue, street, and neighbourhood names unchanged unless an approved authoritative localized form exists.
Maintain one approved bilingual glossary for product terms, geography, filters, source/status labels, privacy, and account language.
Use **neighbourhood** consistently in English product copy unless the approved product style selects another spelling; do not alternate casually with district or area.
Use one approved Dutch equivalent consistently after language review.
Source-provided text must be labeled or presented without implying that MarqtPlaza translated or verified it.
Unknown values must use the approved equivalent of **Unknown** in each locale.
No locale may add stronger verification, freshness, accessibility, or allergy-safety meaning than the source evidence supports.

## Target screens

1. **Localized discovery home:** equivalent value proposition, Hague availability, search, local-only default, and web-results opt-in.
2. **Localized navigation:** text labels, selected language, account entry, and responsive menu.
3. **Localized neighbourhood browse/list:** official names, categories, filters, and result count pluralization.
4. **Localized result card/detail:** field labels, source/date/status, caveats, Unknown, and explicit actions.
5. **Localized map opt-in:** **Show map**, map loading/error, **Show list**, **Use my location**, and manual neighbourhood alternative.
6. **Localized privacy/scope:** geolocation, online search, retention, deletion, and external-source explanations.
7. **Localized account journey:** value, sign-in/register, favourites, session expiry, and deletion.
8. **Localized failure states:** validation, loading, empty, partial, network, permission-denied, and retry.

## Interaction flows

### Flow 1 — Explicit locale switch on homepage

1. **Entry:** Consumer opens the homepage in English.
2. **Action:** Consumer opens the language control and chooses Nederlands.
3. **UI response:** Visible and assistive copy changes atomically without a mixed-language intermediate state.
4. **Success:** The city display changes from The Hague to Den Haag; query and public criteria remain.
5. **Persisted state:** Canonical URL stores validated locale with city, neighbourhood, query, category, filters, and scope.
6. **Back:** Back restores prior locale and criteria consistently with shared history.
7. **Failure:** If a resource fails, show an explicit recoverable error or complete fallback; never silently mix locales.

### Flow 2 — Shared localized search

1. **Entry:** Consumer opens a canonical URL in a fresh session.
2. **Validation:** Locale and public criteria are validated; coordinates, preferences, tokens, and account identifiers are ignored/rejected.
3. **Success:** The requested locale renders while stable entity IDs reproduce the same city/neighbourhood criteria.
4. **Action:** Consumer changes language.
5. **UI response:** Results remain equivalent and only display formatting/copy changes.
6. **Share:** Newly copied URL carries the selected locale and public criteria.
7. **Invalid locale:** Product uses the documented fallback and lets the consumer choose explicitly.

### Flow 3 — Local-only to web-results opt-in

1. **Entry:** A fresh session defaults to local-only in both locales.
2. **Action:** Consumer reads the localized scope explanation and selects **Include web results** or approved Dutch equivalent.
3. **Success:** Local and web results appear in equivalent, programmatically labeled source groups.
4. **Persisted state:** Scope and locale are stored in the canonical URL.
5. **Change/cancel:** Disabling web scope returns to local-only without changing language or other criteria.
6. **Partial error:** The failed source group and Retry action are fully localized.

### Flow 4 — List and map without location dependency

1. **Entry:** Consumer searches or browses a neighbourhood in either locale.
2. **Success:** List results work without map SDK or permission.
3. **Action:** Consumer explicitly selects localized **Show map**.
4. **UI response:** Map resources load only then; loading and failure copy match the active locale.
5. **Location action:** Geolocation prompts only after localized **Use my location**.
6. **Denied:** Localized explanation and manual neighbourhood alternative remain.
7. **Back:** **Show list**/Back preserves locale and criteria.

### Flow 5 — Result trust and action

1. **Entry:** Consumer opens the same result in each locale.
2. **UI response:** Equivalent fields and actions appear in the same information hierarchy.
3. **Trust:** Source name, checked-on date, field status, caveat, and Unknown are localized/formatted without changing evidentiary meaning.
4. **Action:** Consumer follows a valid website, call, directions, share, or favourite action.
5. **Unavailable:** Missing data is explicitly Unknown in the active locale; no dead action appears.
6. **Safety:** Neither locale introduces an unsupported verification badge or allergy-safety guarantee.

### Flow 6 — Account detour

1. **Entry:** Guest selects Favourite in Dutch or English.
2. **UI response:** Equivalent account benefit and account-backed requirement appear; guest browsing remains available.
3. **Action:** Consumer signs in/registers, cancels, or retries after error.
4. **Success/cancel:** Product restores originating criteria, result context, and locale.
5. **Session expiry:** Reauthentication explanation is localized without losing public context.
6. **Persisted state:** Tokens/private preferences never enter the URL.

### Flow 7 — Validation and runtime failure

1. **Entry:** Consumer submits invalid input or a dependency fails.
2. **UI response:** Field errors, summary/status, and recovery action use the selected locale.
3. **Action:** Consumer corrects input or retries.
4. **Success:** Criteria remain and status updates in the same locale.
5. **Fallback:** Missing translation is detected before release; production does not display a raw key as normal content.
6. **Logging:** Error telemetry uses stable codes rather than personal text or locale-dependent parsing.

## Desired and undesired behavior

| Area | Desired behavior | Undesired behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Parity | Same capabilities, fields, and recovery | Feature or state available in one locale only |
| Geography | Den Haag (NL), The Hague (EN) | Random mixing with Haag/Den Hague |
| Locale switch | Preserves criteria and context | Resets search or returns to homepage |
| URL | Validated locale plus public criteria | Locale in token/private-preference URL |
| Fresh session | Local-only in both languages | Different default scope by language |
| Web results | Explicit localized opt-in and groups | Silent blending or untranslated provider status |
| Trust | Equivalent source/date/status/caveat | Stronger claim in one translation |
| Unknown | Explicit localized unknown state | Empty field or invented value |
| Map/location | Explicit localized actions | Auto permission or map blocking list |
| Layout | Handles longer copy and 400% zoom | Truncation, overlap, fixed-width controls |
| Fallback | Whole-screen documented fallback/error | Raw keys or mixed-language fragments |
| External content | Clearly attributed source text | Implied MarqtPlaza translation/verification |

## Required state behavior

### Initial

- Resolve locale using explicit validated URL choice first, then documented user preference/browser fallback as approved.
- Render all above-the-fold copy in one locale.
- State current Hague-only availability using locale-appropriate city name.
- Default fresh-session scope to local-only in both locales.

### Loading

- Localize status text and accessible announcements.
- Preserve criteria and locale.
- Avoid English-only skeleton labels or `aria-label` values.
- Load map resources only after localized explicit **Show map**.

### Success

- Keep capability, result ordering inputs, source groups, fields, and actions equivalent by locale.
- Format dates, times, numbers, and plurals with locale-aware libraries.
- Preserve proper names and evidence meaning.

### Empty

- Explain displayed criteria in the active locale.
- Offer equivalent filter removal, neighbourhood change, and optional web-scope actions.
- Do not silently broaden scope/geography in either locale.

### Error

- Localize validation, network, partial-source, auth, favourite, map, and parameter errors.
- Retain criteria and provide equivalent Retry.
- Do not expose raw localization keys or backend messages.

### Permission denied

- Explain geolocation denial in the active locale.
- Keep manual neighbourhood and list discovery available.
- Do not repeatedly prompt after denial.

### Guest and account

- Guest/account capability boundaries are identical in both locales.
- Favourites remain account-backed only.
- Sign-in, cancel, and expiry restore initiating locale and context.
- Privacy, retention, and deletion content remains semantically equivalent.

## Technical implementation plan

### Discovery and governance

1. Inspect live localization framework, route strategy, server/client rendering, content sources, API localization, and current hard-coded strings.
2. Inventory visible copy, accessible names, metadata, validation, errors, map labels, account states, emails/notifications in scope, and dynamic server messages.
3. Establish an approved bilingual glossary and identify authoritative Hague/neighbourhood names.
4. Define ownership and release workflow without embedding individual attribution in this specification.

### Frontend

5. Replace hard-coded consumer strings with stable semantic message keys.
6. Keep interpolation structured; never concatenate sentence fragments whose grammar changes by locale.
7. Use locale-aware plural, date, time, and number formatting.
8. Preserve stable entity IDs while selecting locale-specific display labels.
9. Implement an accessible language selector that announces its label/current value and restores focus.
10. Update locale and public criteria through shared canonical history.
11. Ensure dialogs, toasts, empty/error states, title/metadata, and accessible-only text use the same locale.
12. Design mobile-first for longer labels without ellipsis hiding essential actions.

### Proposed localization interface

13. Reconcile existing structures before adopting a proposed message catalog keyed by feature and semantic intent.
14. Proposed locale enum is `nl` and `en`; actual URL representation must match inspected routes.
15. Proposed public URL state includes city, neighbourhood, query, category, filters, scope, and locale only.
16. Proposed geography object includes stable `id`, official name, optional approved `displayNameByLocale`, and coverage status.
17. Proposed API errors use stable `code` plus safe parameters; clients own localized messages.
18. Proposed source fields remain source-authored and carry a language tag when known rather than receiving ungoverned translation.

### Integration and validation

19. Validate locale and all URL parameters at client/server boundaries.
20. Ensure API queries use canonical IDs, not translated display labels.
21. Reconcile map-provider locale configuration after explicit map loading; document provider UI outside product control.
22. Add extraction/static checks for missing keys, extra keys, placeholder mismatch, forbidden raw keys, and hard-coded consumer strings where feasible.
23. Add parity fixtures ensuring both locales render every application state.
24. Prevent one locale’s cache from serving another locale’s content through correct cache keys/headers.

### Privacy and accessibility

25. Do not infer sensitive identity attributes from locale or treat locale as consent.
26. Keep locale in public URLs but exclude coordinates, tokens, account IDs, and private preferences.
27. Target WCAG 2.2 AA in both locales for names, roles, focus, status, contrast, reflow, target size, and error identification.
28. Test screen-reader pronunciation/language changes with correct document and inline `lang` attributes.
29. Ensure text zoom, spacing overrides, and mobile reflow handle the longest approved translations.

### Rollout

30. Make translation parity a release gate for every new consumer feature.
31. Run automated catalog checks and manual bilingual functional/content checks before release.
32. Use privacy-approved aggregate missing-key/error telemetry with no query or account data.
33. Roll back both locale versions together if parity or meaning regresses.
34. Treat proposed thresholds, such as zero missing production keys, as delivery targets verified in implementation.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **BR-12-AC-01:** Every in-scope consumer screen and initial/loading/success/empty/error/permission state has complete Dutch and English messages with no raw keys.
2. **BR-12-AC-02:** Functional controls, fields, results, actions, and recovery paths are equivalent in both locales.
3. **BR-12-AC-03:** Dutch consumer copy uses Den Haag and English copy uses The Hague, backed by one stable internal city identifier.
4. **BR-12-AC-04:** An explicit locale switch preserves city, neighbourhood, query, category, filters, scope, and result/account context.
5. **BR-12-AC-05:** Canonical shared URLs validate locale and public criteria and exclude coordinates, private preferences, tokens, and account identifiers.
6. **BR-12-AC-06:** Fresh sessions default to local-only in both locales; web results require explicit localized opt-in and named source groups.
7. **BR-12-AC-07:** List discovery works in both locales without map SDK or permission; map/location load only after their explicit actions.
8. **BR-12-AC-08:** Field-level source/date/status/caveat and Unknown preserve equivalent evidentiary meaning in both locales.
9. **BR-12-AC-09:** Neither locale displays an unsupported verification badge, allergy-safety guarantee, or unsupported coverage claim.
10. **BR-12-AC-10:** Dates, times, numbers, and plurals use correct locale formatting for defined fixtures.
11. **BR-12-AC-11:** Dutch and English journeys meet the WCAG 2.2 AA target, including language metadata and 400% reflow.
12. **BR-12-AC-12:** Account value, guest continuation, account-backed favourites, privacy, retention, and deletion are semantically equivalent.
13. **BR-12-AC-13:** Back/Forward and shared-link restoration preserve locale and validated criteria without mixed-language screens.
14. **BR-12-AC-14:** Current Hague-only availability is explicit in both locales.
