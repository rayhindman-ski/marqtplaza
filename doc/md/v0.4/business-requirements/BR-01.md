# BR-01 — Clear value proposition

## Enhancement assessment

**Priority:** P1 — High

**Criticality:** High

**Complexity:** Low

**Estimated effort:** 3–5 person-days (provisional)

This estimate covers the full existing implementation specification, not only copy: proposition and availability design, bilingual presentation, search-scope explanation, implementation, analytics hooks, accessibility, and acceptance testing. Risk is concentrated in making the global product vision and current Hague availability clear without unsupported comparative, coverage, freshness, or safety claims; complexity remains low because the work primarily composes existing discovery capabilities rather than introducing a new core flow.

- Proposition, content, and bilingual UX design: **1–2 person-days**
- Responsive implementation and integration: **1–2 person-days**
- Accessibility, analytics, and acceptance QA: **1 person-day**

## Exact business requirement

> MarqtPlaza must clearly explain why a consumer should choose it instead of familiar search and map products.

## Objective

Make the homepage answer, before a consumer searches:

1. what MarqtPlaza helps a person discover;
2. why its global discovery platform is useful for finding relevant places in locations the consumer chooses;
3. how local-only and optional web search differ;
4. that discovery works as a list without location access or a map; and
5. what evidence and limitations accompany place information.

The proposition must be specific, concise, and usable in Dutch and English without unsupported superiority, completeness, verification, or safety claims.

**Product vision versus current availability:** MarqtPlaza is intended to be a global website, not a platform focused on one city or one neighbourhood. The current implementation is focused on The Hague. Present that as current availability, not as the product's identity or permanent geographic boundary. Local relevance applies to the location a consumer chooses; it does not restrict the overall platform to that location. Communicating the global vision must not imply that worldwide coverage is already available.

## Scope

- Homepage hero headline, supporting copy, primary action, and secondary explanatory link.
- A short benefits section explaining relevance to a consumer's chosen location, transparent sources, and choice of list or map.
- Search-scope summary adjacent to the search controls.
- A separate current-availability notice reflecting supported locations; currently The Hague.

## Non-goals

- Claiming that MarqtPlaza has more places, better rankings, or fresher data than named competitors.
- Adding unsupported verification badges or guarantees about allergies, dietary safety, opening hours, or availability.
- Implementing result ranking, favourites, registration, or full place-detail trust fields in this requirement.
- Requiring geolocation, location permission, a map SDK, or an account to understand or use the core proposition.
- Launching additional geographic coverage as part of this value-proposition requirement; the global product vision remains in scope for the copy.
- Treating promotional copy as evidence that the promised underlying features already exist.

## Target experience

### Target screens

1. **Homepage, fresh session:** global product value proposition, separate current-availability notice (currently The Hague), local-only search default, list-first discovery, optional “Include web results” control.
2. **Homepage, explanatory panel open:** plain-language comparison of local catalogue and web-result scope, source grouping, and privacy summary.
3. **Results entry:** proposition condensed above or near results, with current criteria and source scope visible.
4. **Narrow/mobile homepage:** same meaning and action order, reflowed without horizontal scrolling.
5. **Dutch locale:** semantically equivalent Dutch copy with no fallback English in the new experience.

### Content requirements

- Lead with a concrete discovery benefit that applies across locations, rather than defining MarqtPlaza as a Hague or single-neighbourhood product.
- Distinguish the global vision from present availability: identify The Hague as the current implementation's supported area without implying that national or worldwide coverage is already live.
- Treat neighbourhood browsing as one way to refine discovery, not as the boundary of the website's purpose.
- Explain that local results are grouped separately from optional web results.
- State that consumers can search and browse neighbourhoods without sharing location.
- Describe source/date/status information as aids to judgement, not guarantees.
- Use one primary call to action; secondary details must not compete visually.
- Do not use “verified,” “safe,” “complete,” “best,” or equivalent claims unless a separately approved, field-level policy supports them.

## Numbered interaction flows

### Flow 1 — Enter through the homepage and search locally

1. **Entry:** A fresh, signed-out or signed-in consumer opens the homepage.
2. **UI state:** The global product proposition, separate current-availability notice, query field, and local-only default are visible without interaction.
3. **Action:** The consumer enters a query and activates the primary search action.
4. **UI response:** A progress state identifies that local results are loading; the page does not initialize a map SDK.
5. **Success:** The list results screen preserves query, city, locale, and local scope.
6. **Persisted state:** The canonical URL contains only eligible shareable criteria.
7. **Back:** Browser Back restores the homepage criteria and scroll/focus context where technically feasible.
8. **Retry:** A failed request presents an inline retry that retains entered criteria.

### Flow 2 — Understand the difference from generic search

1. **Entry:** The consumer encounters the benefits summary in the homepage.
2. **Action:** The consumer opens “Why MarqtPlaza?” or equivalent explanatory content.
3. **UI response:** An inline panel or page explains the global discovery vision, relevance to the consumer's chosen location, optional neighbourhood browsing, list-first use, source groups, and evidence limitations. It distinguishes current Hague availability from future geographic coverage.
4. **Success:** The consumer can return to the unchanged search state.
5. **Cancel/back:** Closing the panel or using Back returns focus to the trigger.
6. **Persisted state:** Opening the explanation does not alter search scope or consent.

### Flow 3 — Opt into web results

1. **Entry:** The consumer sees local-only selected in a fresh session.
2. **Action:** The consumer selects “Include web results.”
3. **UI response:** Nearby copy explains that an additional source group will be queried and shown separately.
4. **Confirmation:** Search scope changes only after the explicit selection.
5. **Success:** Results identify local and web groups independently.
6. **Cancel:** Deselecting the option returns to local-only and does not remove query text.
7. **Persisted state:** Scope is represented in the canonical URL; private preferences and tokens are excluded.

### Flow 4 — Choose list, map, or manual neighbourhood

1. **Entry:** The consumer starts from the proposition or results.
2. **Action:** The consumer browses the list or selects a neighbourhood manually.
3. **UI response:** Discovery works without location permission and without loading a map SDK.
4. **Optional map:** The map loads only after explicit “Show map” activation.
5. **Optional location:** Geolocation is requested only after explicit “Use my location” activation.
6. **Permission denied:** The interface retains query and offers manual neighbourhood selection.

## Shared URL and state rules

- The proposed canonical shareable state supports `city`, `neighbourhood`, `query`, `category`, `filters`, `scope`, and `locale`.
- Coordinates, location precision, private preferences, account identifiers, and authentication tokens must never appear in the shared URL.
- Parameters must be allow-listed, length-bounded, normalized, and validated before use.
- Invalid parameters must produce a recoverable explanation and safe defaults, not a blank or broken screen.
- Browser history must preserve meaningful criteria changes and support Back/Forward without duplicate searches where cached data remains valid.
- The exact URL shape must be reconciled with existing live routes and contracts during implementation.

## State requirements

| State | Required proposed behavior |
|---|---|
| Initial | Global product proposition and separate current availability are visible; search is local-only; no map SDK or geolocation request occurs. |
| Loading | Search controls remain understandable; progress is announced; criteria remain visible; no false result count is shown. |
| Success | Results retain the concise proposition and expose criteria and source groups. |
| Empty | Explain that no matching local result was found; offer query/category/neighbourhood changes and optional web opt-in. |
| Error | Identify the failed operation, preserve input, and provide retry; do not imply that no places exist. |
| Permission denied | Continue list discovery and offer manual neighbourhood selection; do not repeatedly prompt. |
| Guest | Full browsing and searching remain available; account-only benefits are clearly separated. |
| Account | Same proposition and discovery access; account status must not alter the truth of availability or source claims. |

## Desired and undesired outcomes

| Desired behavior | Undesired behavior |
|---|---|
| A concrete discovery benefit applicable across locations appears before search. | Positioning the overall website as Hague-only or focused on one neighbourhood. |
| Local-only is the proposed fresh-session default. | “Also search online” silently enabled or presented as consent already given. |
| Optional web results are separated by source group. | Local and web results blended without provenance. |
| List discovery works without map or permission. | Search blocked until map initialization or location approval. |
| Copy presents evidence fields with limits. | Verification badges or allergy-safety guarantees without support. |
| The global vision and current supported locations are clearly distinguished. | Claiming worldwide coverage is already live, or treating the current Hague implementation as a permanent product boundary. |
| Guest browsing remains useful. | Registration wall before search or neighbourhood browsing. |
| Dutch and English convey the same meaning. | Partial translation, language mixing, or materially different promises. |

## Proposed technical implementation

### Frontend

1. Inspect the live consumer stack, routing, localization, design tokens, and current homepage composition before choosing components.
2. Introduce a semantic hero with one `h1`, globally applicable supporting copy, a separate current-availability note, and primary search action. Keep availability wording separate from the core proposition so it can change as geographic coverage expands.
3. Add a reusable proposition/benefit component suitable for homepage and condensed results contexts.
4. Implement local-only as explicit initial scope in fresh-session state.
5. Ensure explanatory disclosure is keyboard operable, focus-managed, and non-modal unless product constraints require a dialog.
6. Keep list discovery independent from all map packages; dynamically import map code only after “Show map.”
7. Preserve criteria through navigation using validated route state rather than hidden global state alone.
8. Add localized strings in Dutch and English as one atomic change.

### Proposed data and API contracts

- No new endpoint is inherently required to render static proposition copy.
- Proposed search request shape: `{ city, neighbourhood?, query?, category?, filters?, scope, locale }`.
- Proposed `scope` values: `local` and `local_web`; these names are illustrative and must be reconciled with existing contracts.
- Proposed result response groups: `{ local: ResultGroup, web?: ResultGroup, notices: Notice[] }`.
- Source/date/status fields must be field-level where available; unknown values must be rendered as unknown, not inferred.
- Existing APIs, telemetry schemas, caching, and error formats must be inspected rather than assumed.

### Integration and analytics

- Integrate with BR-02 navigation wording, BR-03 list-first discovery, and BR-04 search-scope disclosure.
- Emit privacy-minimized events for proposition explanation opened, search started, scope selected, and map explicitly requested.
- Do not send raw query text, coordinates, tokens, or private preference values in analytics.
- Define event purpose, retention, access, and consent basis with the privacy implementation before release.

### Privacy and accessibility

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA.
- Keep reading order aligned with visual order; provide visible focus and a descriptive accessible name for every action.
- Ensure text and controls meet applicable contrast, target-size, zoom, and reflow requirements.
- Do not request geolocation on load or as a side effect of search.
- Provide a manual neighbourhood alternative adjacent to location actions.
- Make status and scope changes perceivable without relying only on color.

### Rollout

1. Approve English and Dutch copy together.
2. Validate prohibited claims and the distinction between global product vision and current availability; check that neither the hero nor the explanation defines the product as limited to one city or neighbourhood.
3. Release behind an existing feature mechanism if one exists; do not invent a second flag system.
4. Conduct keyboard, screen-reader, 320 CSS-pixel, 200% zoom, slow-network, and failure-state checks.
5. Compare comprehension and search-start signals without collecting query content.
6. Provide rollback to prior copy/layout while preserving validated canonical URLs.

## Dependencies

- **BR-02:** navigation labels must reinforce, not contradict, the proposition.
- **BR-03:** list and neighbourhood discovery must substantiate the non-map promise.
- **BR-04:** transparent local/web scope is part of the proposition.
- **BR-05:** current Hague neighbourhoods and categories demonstrate location-specific relevance; they must not redefine the global product proposition as city-specific.
- **BR-06:** accessibility requirements govern all proposition controls.
- **BR-07:** trust and freshness fields constrain evidence claims.
- **BR-08:** privacy language governs scope, analytics, and location explanations.
- **BR-09:** mobile reflow must preserve hierarchy.
- **BR-10:** result actions must deliver on the promised discovery path.
- **BR-11:** guest/account copy must not turn the proposition into an account gate.
- **BR-12:** Dutch-English parity applies to every new string.

## Measurable acceptance criteria

1. **BR-01-AC-01:** A fresh homepage displays a discovery proposition applicable across locations, communicates the global product vision, separately identifies current availability (currently The Hague), and shows one primary search action before any user interaction. It does not define the website as focused on a single city or neighbourhood.
2. **BR-01-AC-02:** Fresh-session search scope is local-only, with web results requiring explicit opt-in.
3. **BR-01-AC-03:** Explanatory content distinguishes local and web source groups without unsupported coverage or quality claims.
4. **BR-01-AC-04:** Search and neighbourhood browsing operate without requesting geolocation or loading the map SDK.
5. **BR-01-AC-05:** “Show map” is the only action that initiates map loading in the covered discovery flow.
6. **BR-01-AC-06:** “Use my location” is the only action that initiates geolocation permission, and denial leaves a manual alternative.
7. **BR-01-AC-07:** Canonical URLs include only validated shareable criteria and exclude coordinates, private preferences, identifiers, and tokens.
8. **BR-01-AC-08:** Empty and error states preserve criteria and present at least one relevant recovery action.
9. **BR-01-AC-09:** Guest consumers can search and browse without registration.
10. **BR-01-AC-10:** No new copy promises unsupported verification, allergy safety, completeness, or live coverage outside currently supported locations. Global ambition is clearly distinguished from coverage available today.
11. **BR-01-AC-11:** New English and Dutch content has equivalent intent, actions, caveats, geographical terminology, and distinction between global vision and current availability.
12. **BR-01-AC-12:** The target experience passes agreed WCAG 2.2 AA checks for semantics, keyboard, focus, contrast, zoom, and reflow.
